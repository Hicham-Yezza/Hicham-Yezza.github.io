Work

My work is about evidence: defining what good performance looks like for an AI system in a real setting, testing for it, and turning the results into decisions people can act on.

AI evaluation

At the BBC I lead work on evaluating AI systems for accuracy, editorial risk and safety. The evaluations inform decisions about whether and how tools are used. Much of the work is translation in both directions: turning editorial standards into criteria a system can be tested against, and turning technical evidence into guidance that editors and senior leaders can use.

I design evaluation suites and harnesses for tools built on large language models, combine expert human judgement with automated assessment, and check automated evaluators against human reviewers. A growing part of the work is multilingual: how well AI systems perform outside English.

News integrity in AI assistants

  • News Integrity in AI Assistants Toolkit

    BBC and EBU, 2025. Lead author, with James Fletcher and Dorien Verckist.

    Sets out the ingredients of a good AI assistant news answer, covering accuracy, sourcing, quotes, fact and opinion, and context, alongside a taxonomy of failures. It is written for technology companies, media organisations, researchers and trainers.

  • News Integrity in AI Assistants

    BBC and EBU, 2025. Contributor.

    The study behind the Toolkit: journalists from 22 public-service media organisations in 18 countries assessed AI assistants' answers to news questions in 14 languages.

  • Towards an AI-in-News Evaluation Benchmark

    EBU Data Technology Seminar, 2026. Co-presenter.

    How the Toolkit is becoming a repeatable evaluation method. I now lead joint BBC and EBU work on that capability.

Research

  • Towards Neuro-Symbolic AI: Knowledge Graph-Enhanced LLM Summarisation

    MSc dissertation, University of Reading, 2024

    Compared standard summarisation models with variants that draw on knowledge graphs, evaluated with established metrics and a new measure of consistency with the graph.
