About me.

I'm a multidisciplinary leader with more than two decades of experience across policy, technology and media. I now lead Responsible AI evaluation at the BBC, and I'm a Visiting Fellow at the University of Reading.

My work at the BBC centres on how AI systems handle information. I'm lead author of the BBC/EBU News Integrity in AI Assistants Toolkit, and I lead joint work with the EBU to turn it into a scalable evaluation capability for the news industry. Before that I was a geopolitical analyst at BBC Monitoring, where I helped lead the organisation's move into data and AI.

For nineteen years I was founder and editor-in-chief of Ceasefire Magazine, and I've written for The Guardian, openDemocracy and Le Monde diplomatique. As a consultant I delivered major projects for UN agencies, the WHO and the European Union, and wrote the official UN guidance on partnering in Small Island Developing States.

I trained as a computer scientist at the University of Nottingham, and later returned to the field with an MSc in Data Science and Advanced Computing from Reading.

Most of my working life has gone into work with a public purpose. I wrote and spoke widely on civil liberties, academic freedom, refugee rights and penal reform, including at events hosted by Amnesty International, Liberty and the LSE, in the House of Lords and at the United Nations.
