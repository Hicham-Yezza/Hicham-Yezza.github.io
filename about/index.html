About Hicham Yezza

I'm Principal Data Scientist for Responsible AI at the BBC, where I lead work on evaluating the accuracy, risks and safety of AI systems, and their impact on the BBC's editorial and public-service values.

Biography

I'm the lead author of the BBC/EBU News Integrity in AI Assistants Toolkit, and I lead joint BBC and EBU work to turn it into a repeatable way of evaluating AI news answers. I'm also a Visiting Fellow at the University of Reading.

I joined the BBC in 2021 at BBC Monitoring, working on the Middle East and North Africa from Arabic, French and English sources, and later helped launch and lead its Data & AI hub as a Senior Journalist.

Before the BBC I spent two decades as an editor and writer. I founded Ceasefire Magazine, an independent magazine of politics and culture, in 2002, and was its editor-in-chief until 2021. I set its editorial standards and house style, and edited every article it published online, more than 1,300 in all. I wrote for The Guardian, openDemocracy and Le Monde diplomatique, and worked as an editorial consultant on Al Jazeera English programmes.

Alongside that I worked as a technical and research consultant on sustainable development, for UN agencies, the World Health Organization, the European Commission and UK government departments. As a Senior Research Associate at The Partnering Initiative, I was lead author of the official UN guidance on partnerships in Small Island Developing States.

I trained as a computer scientist at the University of Nottingham, and later completed an MSc in Data Science and Advanced Computing at Reading, with Distinction. I work in English, French and Arabic.
