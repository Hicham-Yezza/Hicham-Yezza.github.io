About me.

I'm a multidisciplinary leader with more than two decades of experience across policy, technology and media. I now lead Responsible AI evaluation at the BBC, and I'm a Visiting Fellow at the University of Reading.

Biography

As Principal Data Scientist for Responsible AI at the BBC, I lead work on evaluating the risks, safety and public value of AI systems, and help shape how the BBC approaches responsible AI across the organisation. I'm lead author of the BBC/EBU News Integrity in AI Assistants Toolkit, and lead joint BBC and EBU work to turn it into a scalable evaluation capability for the news industry.

I joined the BBC in 2021 as a geopolitical analyst at BBC Monitoring. I later helped launch and lead its Data & AI hub, and played a key part in BBC Monitoring's editorial and strategic transformation into a data-driven, AI-fluent organisation.

For nineteen years I was founder and editor-in-chief of Ceasefire Magazine, an independent, reader-supported publication with readers in more than 100 countries. I led an international team of writers, editors and designers, and published hundreds of contributors from more than two dozen countries. I've written for The Guardian, openDemocracy and Le Monde diplomatique, and provided editorial and research input to Al Jazeera English programmes including The Listening Post and People & Power.

As a consultant, I've delivered projects principally in sustainable development for institutional clients including the World Health Organization, the European Commission, the UK Foreign Office and DFID. At The Partnering Initiative I wrote the official UN guidance on partnering in Small Island Developing States. I've also provided academic and editorial input on hundreds of research papers, doctoral dissertations and policy reports.

I studied computer science at the University of Nottingham, where I also did doctoral research on data compression. I later returned to the field with an MSc in Data Science and Advanced Computing from Reading, completed with Distinction.
